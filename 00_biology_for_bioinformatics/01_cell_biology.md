# Cell Biology

## 1. Introduction to Cell Biology

### 1.1 What is Cell Biology?

Cell biology is the study of the structure, organization, function, communication, reproduction, and regulation of cells.

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.Cells perform essential activities such as metabolism, growth, reproduction, and response to the environment.

### 1.2 Cell Theory

The classical cell theory states:

1. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells.
2. The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells.

Modern cell theory additionally recognizes that:

- Cells contain hereditary information.
- Energy flow occurs within cells.
- Cells share fundamental chemical components.
- Cellular activities determine the functions of organisms.

### 1.3 Types of Cells

Cells are broadly divided into:

- Prokaryotic cells
- Eukaryotic cells

#### Prokaryotic Cells

**Examples:**

- Bacteria
- Archaea

**Characteristics:**

- No membrane-bound nucleus
- DNA is usually located in a nucleoid region
- Generally smaller than eukaryotic cells
- Usually contain a single circular chromosome
- May contain plasmids
- Lack membrane-bound organelles
- Contain ribosomes

#### Eukaryotic Cells

**Examples:**

- Animal cells
- Plant cells
- Fungal cells
- Protist cells

**Characteristics:**

- Membrane-bound nucleus
- Linear chromosomes
- Membrane-bound organelles
- Larger and structurally more complex
- 80S cytoplasmic ribosomes

**Basic structure of a eukaryotic cell:**
- Cell membrane – controls movement of substances
- Cytoplasm – cytosol + organelles
- Nucleus – contains DNA, controls gene expression
- Mitochondria – produce ATP
- Ribosomes – synthesize proteins
- Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) – protein/lipid synthesis
- Golgi apparatus – modifies, sorts, packages proteins/lipids
- Lysosomes – digest waste/damaged components
- Cytoskeleton – shape, movement, intracellular transport


### 1.4 Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

| Feature | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Absent | Present |
| DNA | Usually circular | Usually linear |
| Chromosomes | Usually one main chromosome | Multiple chromosomes |
| Membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present |
| Ribosome | 70S | 80S |
| Cell size | Usually smaller | Usually larger |
| Cell division | Binary fission | Mitosis/meiosis |
| Examples | Bacteria, Archaea | Animals, plants, fungi |

## 2. Cell Structure

### 2.1 Plasma Membrane/Cell Membrane

The plasma membrane separates the cell from its external environment.

#### Structure

The membrane mainly consists of:

- Phospholipids
- Cholesterol
- Membrane proteins
- Carbohydrates

The membrane follows the fluid mosaic model (fluid = lateral movement; mosaic = distributed components).

#### Functions

- Maintains cellular integrity
- Controls movement of substances
- Cell signaling
- Cell recognition
- Cell adhesion
- Maintains ion gradients
- Transport: passive (diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis) vs active (ATP-dependent, e.g. Na+/K+ pump)


### 2.2 Cytoplasm

The cytoplasm includes:

- Cytosol
- Organelles
- Cytoplasmic structures

Many metabolic reactions occur in the cytoplasm.


### 2.3 Nucleus

The nucleus contains most of the genetic material in eukaryotic cells.

#### Components

- Nuclear envelope
- Nuclear pores
- Nucleoplasm
- Chromatin
- Nucleolus

#### Functions

- DNA storage
- DNA replication
- Transcription
- Regulation of gene expression

---

## 3. Cell Organelles

### 3.1 Mitochondria

Mitochondria are major sites of cellular energy production. Its called Powerhouse of the cell.

#### Structure

- Outer membrane
- Intermembrane space
- Inner membrane
- Cristae
- Matrix

#### Functions

- ATP production
- TCA cycle
- Oxidative phosphorylation
- Regulation of apoptosis
- Metabolic signaling
- Ca²⁺ homeostasis

Mitochondria contain their own genome.

#### Endosymbiotic Theory

- Mitochondria evolved from engulfed aerobic bacteria
- Evidence: own circular DNA, own ribosomes, binary-fission-like division, double membrane, bacterial genetic similarity — "semi-autonomous"


### 3.2 Ribosomes

Ribosomes are responsible for protein synthesis.

#### Prokaryotic Ribosome

- 70S
- 30S small subunit
- 50S large subunit

#### Eukaryotic Cytoplasmic Ribosome

- 80S
- 40S small subunit
- 60S large subunit

#### Function

- Ribosomes translate mRNA into protein.
- Stages: Initiation → Elongation → Termination
- Free ribosomes → cytosolic proteins; 
- ER-bound → secreted/membrane proteins


### 3.3 Endoplasmic Reticulum

Two major types:

#### Rough ER

Contains ribosomes.

##### Functions:

- Protein synthesis
- Protein folding
- Initial protein modification

#### Smooth ER

##### Functions:

- Lipid synthesis
- Steroid synthesis
- Detoxification
- Calcium storage


### 3.4 Golgi Apparatus

- Cisternae: cis → medial → trans

#### Functions:

- Protein modification
- Protein sorting
- Protein packaging
- Vesicle formation
- transports proteins/lipids

Proteins synthesized in the ER may be transported to the Golgi apparatus for further processing.


### 3.5 Lysosomes

Lysosomes contain hydrolytic enzymes, acidic interior.

#### Functions:

- Breakdown of macromolecules
- Digests proteins, lipids, carbs, nucleic acids, debris
- Recycling of cellular components

### 3.6 Peroxisomes

#### Functions:

- Fatty acid oxidation
- Detoxification
- Hydrogen peroxide metabolism

Catalase is an important peroxisomal enzyme.


### 3.7 Cytoskeleton

The cytoskeleton maintains cellular shape and organization.

| Component | Protein | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Microfilaments | Actin | Movement, shape, contraction, cytokinesis |
| Intermediate filaments | Varies | Mechanical strength |
| Microtubules | Tubulin | Transport, chromosome movement, spindle |


### 3.8 Difference between Plant cell & Animal Cell

| Feature | Plant Cell | Animal Cell |
|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | Present | Absent |
| Chloroplast | Present | Absent |
| Large central vacuole | Usually present | Usually absent |
| Centrioles | Usually absent in higher plants | Present |
| Shape | More rigid | More flexible |

#### Plant-specific structures

- Cell wall
- Chloroplast
- Large central vacuole

## 4. Cell Cycle

The cell cycle is the series of events through which a cell grows, replicates its DNA, and divides into daughter cells. Two major phases:

- Interphase: G1 (growth) → S (DNA replication) → G2 (prep for mitosis)
- M Phase: Mitosis + Cytokinesis
- Regulated by cyclins, CDKs, checkpoints

### G1 Phase

- Cell growth
- Protein synthesis
- Organelle production

### S Phase

DNA replication occurs.

### G2 Phase

- Further growth
- Preparation for cell division
- DNA quality control

### M Phase

Includes:

- Mitosis
- Cytokinesis

---

## 5. Mitosis

Mitosis produces two genetically similar daughter cells.

### Stages:

1. Prophase
2. Prometaphase
3. Metaphase
4. Anaphase
5. Telophase
6. Cytokinesis


#### Prophase

- Chromatin condenses
- Chromosomes become visible
- Mitotic spindle begins to form

#### Metaphase

Chromosomes align at the metaphase plate.

#### Anaphase

Sister chromatids separate.

#### Telophase

Nuclear envelopes reform around the separated chromosomes.

#### Cytokinesis

The cytoplasm divides.

---

## 6. Meiosis

Meiosis is a specialized cell division that Produces 4 haploid, genetically varied cells.

It consists of:

- Meiosis I
- Meiosis II

### Meiosis I (reductional, crossing over)

Homologous chromosomes separate.

### Meiosis II (like mitosis)

Sister chromatids separate.

### Biological Importance of Meiosis:

- Reduces chromosome number
- Produces genetic variation
- Enables sexual reproduction

### Major sources of variation include:

- Crossing over
- Independent assortment
- Random fertilization

### Mitosis vs Meiosis
| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Divisions | 1 | 2 |
| Daughter cells | 2 | 4 |
| Chromosome number | Maintained | Halved |
| Genetic variation | Limited | High |

---

## 7. Cell-Cycle Regulation

Cell-cycle progression is controlled by regulatory proteins.

Important regulators:

- Cyclins
- Cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs)
- Tumor suppressors
- Checkpoint proteins

Important checkpoints:
- G1/S checkpoint — cell size, nutrients, DNA damage
- G2/M checkpoint — replication completeness, DNA damage
- Spindle assembly checkpoint — kinetochore attachment
- Regulators: Cyclins, CDKs, p53, Rb

### Biological Importance

Cell-cycle dysregulation can contribute to uncontrolled cell proliferation.

## 8. Cell–Cell Communication/Cell Signaling

Cell signaling allows cells to detect and respond to external and internal signals.

### Basic signaling process:

```text
Ligand
  ↓
Signal
  ↓
Receptor
  ↓
Signal transduction
  ↓
Intracellular signaling molecules
  ↓
Cellular response
```

### Major Receptor Types
- G-protein-coupled receptors(Ligand → GPCR → G protein → Adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA)
- Receptor tyrosine kinases(Growth factor → RTK → Ras → Raf → MEK → ERK → Gene expression)
- Cytokine receptors
- Intracellular receptors

### Second Messengers
Important second messengers include:
- cAMP
- Ca²⁺
- IP3
- DAG
They transmit signals from receptors to intracellular targets.
- Phosphorylation (kinase adds) vs dephosphorylation (phosphatase removes)


### Major Signaling Pathways

#### MAPK Pathway
Important for:
- Cell proliferation
- Differentiation
- Survival

#### PI3K/AKT Pathway
Important for:
- Cell survival
- Growth
- Metabolism

#### JAK/STAT Pathway
Important for:
- Cytokine signaling
- Immune responses
- Cell growth and differentiation

### Cells communicate through:
- Direct contact
- Paracrine signaling
- Autocrine signaling
- Endocrine signaling
- Synaptic signaling

### Cell communication regulates:
- Growth
- Differentiation
- Immune responses
- Metabolism
- Tissue organization

## 9. Cell Differentiation
Cell differentiation is the process by which cells acquire specialized characteristics.
- Same DNA → differential gene expression → different cell types

### Examples:
- Stem cell → neuron
- Stem cell → muscle cell
- Hematopoietic stem cell → immune cell

### Differentiation is controlled by:
- Transcription factors
- Signaling pathways
- Epigenetic mechanisms
- Gene regulatory networks
---

## 10. Stem Cells
Stem cells have the ability to:
- Self-renew
- Differentiate into specialized cell types

### Major categories:

| Potency | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Totipotent | All cell types + extraembryonic | Zygote |
| Pluripotent | All 3 germ layers | Embryonic stem cells |
| Multipotent | Related lineage cell types | Hematopoietic stem cells |
| Unipotent | One cell type | — |

### Examples:
- Embryonic stem cells
- Adult stem cells
- Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs): reprogrammed via Yamanaka factors (OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, c-MYC)
- Germ layers: Ectoderm (nervous system, skin), Mesoderm (muscle, bone, blood), Endoderm (liver, pancreas, gut)

## 11. Cell Death

### 11.1 Apoptosis

Apoptosis is regulated programmed cell death.
intrinsic (mitochondrial/cytochrome c) or extrinsic (death receptor) pathway → caspases


Characteristics:

- Cell shrinkage
- Chromatin condensation
- DNA fragmentation
- Formation of apoptotic bodies
- Generally limited inflammatory response

Important proteins:

- Caspases
- BCL-2 family proteins


### 11.2 Necrosis

Necrosis is associated with uncontrolled cellular injury and loss of membrane integrity.

Characteristics may include:

- Cell swelling
- Membrane disruption
- Release of cellular contents
- Triggers inflammation


### 11.3 Apoptosis vs Necrosis


| Feature | Apoptosis | Necrosis |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Programmed | Uncontrolled |
| Cell size | Shrinks | Swells |
| Inflammation | Minimal | Significant |


### 11.4 Autophagy

Autophagy is a cellular recycling process in which cellular components are delivered to lysosomes for degradation.

It can contribute to:

- Cellular quality control
- Nutrient recycling
- Stress adaptation

### 11.5 Endocytosis

The cell takes substances into the cell using membrane vesicles.

Types:

- Phagocytosis
- Pinocytosis
- Receptor-mediated endocytosis


### 11.6 Exocytosis

The cell releases substances through vesicles that fuse with the plasma membrane.

Examples:

- Hormone secretion
- Neurotransmitter release
- Enzyme secretion

## 12. Cell Membrane and Transport

### 12.1 Passive Transport

Passive transport does not directly require cellular energy.

Types:

- Simple diffusion
- Facilitated diffusion
- Osmosis

#### Simple Diffusion

Movement of molecules from higher concentration to lower concentration.

#### Facilitated Diffusion

Transport occurs through membrane proteins.

#### Osmosis

Movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane.


### 12.2 Active Transport

Active transport requires energy, commonly ATP.

Examples:

- Sodium-potassium pump
- Calcium pumps
- Proton pumps

#### Sodium-Potassium Pump

The Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase maintains ion gradients across the plasma membrane.
