## 1. Cell Theory & Cell Structure

**Cell theory**: all living organisms are composed of cells, the basic structural and functional unit of life, and new cells arise from pre-existing cells. 

**Cells**: A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. All living organisms are made up of cells. Cells perform essential activities such as metabolism, growth, reproduction, and response to the environment.

**Basic structure of a eukaryotic cell:**
- Cell membrane – controls movement of substances
- Cytoplasm – cytosol + organelles
- Nucleus – contains DNA, controls gene expression
- Mitochondria – produce ATP
- Ribosomes – synthesize proteins
- Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) – protein/lipid synthesis
- Golgi apparatus – modifies, sorts, packages proteins/lipids
- Lysosomes – digest waste/damaged components
- Cytoskeleton – shape, movement, intracellular transport

## 2. Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cell

| Feature | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Absent | Present |
| DNA | Usually circular | Usually linear |
| Membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present |
| Size | Smaller | Larger |
| Ribosome | 70S | 80S |
| Cell division | Binary fission | Mitosis/meiosis |

## 3. Cell Membrane
- Composition: phospholipids, proteins, cholesterol, carbohydrates
- Fluid Mosaic Model (fluid = lateral movement; mosaic = distributed components)
- Functions: integrity, transport control, signaling, recognition, adhesion, homeostasis
- Transport: passive (diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis) vs active (ATP-dependent, e.g. Na+/K+ pump)

## 4. Nucleus
- Structure: nuclear envelope, nuclear pores, nucleoplasm, chromatin, nucleolus
- Functions: stores DNA, replication, transcription, gene regulation

## 5. Mitochondria
- "Powerhouse of the cell" — ATP production, oxidative phosphorylation, TCA cycle, Ca²⁺ homeostasis, apoptosis regulation
- **Endosymbiotic Theory**: mitochondria evolved from engulfed aerobic bacteria
- Evidence: own circular DNA, own ribosomes, binary-fission-like division, double membrane, bacterial genetic similarity — "semi-autonomous"

## 6. Ribosome
- rRNA + proteins; eukaryotes 80S (40S+60S), prokaryotes 70S (30S+50S)
- Function: translation (mRNA → protein)
- Free ribosomes → cytosolic proteins; ER-bound → secreted/membrane proteins
- Stages: Initiation → Elongation → Termination

## 7. Endoplasmic Reticulum
- Rough ER (ribosomes) — protein synthesis/folding/QC
- Smooth ER (no ribosomes) — lipid synthesis, detox, Ca²⁺ storage

## 8. Golgi Apparatus
- Cisternae: cis → medial → trans
- Function: modifies → sorts → packages → transports proteins/lipids

## 9. Lysosome
- Hydrolytic enzymes, acidic interior
- Digests proteins, lipids, carbs, nucleic acids, debris
- Role in autophagy

## 10. Cytoskeleton

| Component | Protein | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Microfilaments | Actin | Movement, shape, contraction, cytokinesis |
| Intermediate filaments | Varies | Mechanical strength |
| Microtubules | Tubulin | Transport, chromosome movement, spindle |

## 11. Cell Cycle
The cell cycle is the series of events through which a cell grows, replicates its DNA, and divides into daughter cells. Two major phases:
- Interphase: G1 (growth) → S (DNA replication) → G2 (prep for mitosis)
- M Phase: Mitosis + Cytokinesis
- Regulated by cyclins, CDKs, checkpoints

## 12. Mitosis
- Prophase → Prometaphase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase → Cytokinesis
- Produces 2 genetically similar daughter cells

## 13. Meiosis
- Meiosis I (reductional, crossing over) → Meiosis II (like mitosis)
- Produces 4 haploid, genetically varied cells

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Divisions | 1 | 2 |
| Daughter cells | 2 | 4 |
| Chromosome number | Maintained | Halved |
| Genetic variation | Limited | High |

## 14. Cell Cycle Checkpoints
- G1/S checkpoint — cell size, nutrients, DNA damage
- G2/M checkpoint — replication completeness, DNA damage
- Spindle assembly checkpoint — kinetochore attachment
- Regulators: Cyclins, CDKs, p53, Rb

## 15. Apoptosis vs Necrosis
- Apoptosis: programmed, controlled — intrinsic (mitochondrial/cytochrome c) or extrinsic (death receptor) pathway → caspases
- Necrosis: injury-caused, uncontrolled, triggers inflammation

| Feature | Apoptosis | Necrosis |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Programmed | Uncontrolled |
| Cell size | Shrinks | Swells |
| Inflammation | Minimal | Significant |

## 16. Cell Signaling
- Pathway: Ligand → Receptor → Signal transduction → Cellular response
- Types: autocrine, paracrine, endocrine, synaptic, juxtacrine
- **GPCR**: Ligand → GPCR → G protein → Adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA
- **RTK**: Growth factor → RTK → Ras → Raf → MEK → ERK → Gene expression
- Second messengers: cAMP, Ca²⁺, IP₃, DAG
- Phosphorylation (kinase adds) vs dephosphorylation (phosphatase removes)

## 17. Cell Differentiation & Stem Cells
- Same DNA → differential gene expression → different cell types
- Stem cell properties: self-renewal + differentiation

| Potency | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Totipotent | All cell types + extraembryonic | Zygote |
| Pluripotent | All 3 germ layers | Embryonic stem cells |
| Multipotent | Related lineage cell types | Hematopoietic stem cells |
| Unipotent | One cell type | — |

- iPSCs: reprogrammed via Yamanaka factors (OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, c-MYC)
- Germ layers: Ectoderm (nervous system, skin), Mesoderm (muscle, bone, blood), Endoderm (liver, pancreas, gut)
