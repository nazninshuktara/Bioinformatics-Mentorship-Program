# Microbiology

## 1. Overview & Scope

**Microbiology** is the branch of biology that studies microorganisms, including bacteria, viruses, fungi, and other microscopic organisms.

Microorganisms play important roles in:

- Human health and disease
- Environment
- Biotechnology
- Agriculture
- Evolution
- Industrial processes


**Scope:**

- Microbial structure and classification
- Microbial growth and metabolism
- Microbial genetics
- Host-microbe interactions
- Pathogenesis
- Antimicrobial resistance
- Microbial genomics
- Metagenomics


**Applications:**

- Infectious disease research
- Vaccine development
- Antibiotic discovery
- Pathogen identification
- Microbiome research
- Bioinformatics analysis

# 3. Bacteria

Bacteria are single-celled prokaryotic organisms without a membrane-bound nucleus.

General features:

- Circular chromosome
- 70S ribosome
- Cell wall containing peptidoglycan
- Reproduce mainly by binary fission


Basic bacterial structure:
```
Capsule
   ↓
Cell Wall
   ↓
Cell Membrane
   ↓
Cytoplasm
   ↓
Nucleoid DNA
```


---

# 4. Bacterial Cell Structure

## Cell Wall

Functions:

- Maintains cell shape
- Provides protection
- Prevents osmotic damage


## Cell Membrane

Functions:

- Controls transport
- Energy production
- Signal exchange


## Capsule

A protective outer layer made of polysaccharides.

Functions:

- Helps bacterial survival
- Protects against immune responses


## Flagella

Structures responsible for bacterial movement.


## Pili / Fimbriae

Functions:

- Attachment to host cells
- DNA transfer during conjugation


## Plasmids

Small circular DNA molecules separate from bacterial chromosome.

Functions:

- Carry antibiotic resistance genes
- Transfer genetic information


---

# 5. Bacterial Classification

Bacteria are classified based on:

- Cell morphology
- Staining characteristics
- Genetic relationship


## Based on Shape

| Shape | Example |
|---|---|
| Cocci | *Staphylococcus* |
| Bacilli | *E. coli* |
| Spiral | *Treponema* |


## Gram Staining

Gram staining differentiates bacteria based on cell wall structure.


### Gram-positive Bacteria

Characteristics:

- Thick peptidoglycan layer
- Retain crystal violet stain


### Gram-negative Bacteria

Characteristics:

- Thin peptidoglycan layer
- Outer membrane present
- Contains lipopolysaccharide (LPS)

- Nutrient availability
- Oxygen availability

Lag Phase
     ↓
Log Phase
     ↓
Stationary Phase
     ↓
Death Phase

## Culture Media

Used for growing microorganisms.

Types:

- General media
- Selective media
- Differential media

# 7. Microbial Metabolism

Microorganisms obtain energy through different metabolic pathways.


## Aerobic Respiration

Requires oxygen.


## Anaerobic Respiration

Occurs without oxygen.


## Fermentation

Energy production without external electron acceptors.


Microbial metabolism influences:

- Growth
- Virulence
- Environmental adaptation

# 8. Microbial Genetics

Microbial genetics studies genetic information and variation in microorganisms.


Bacterial genetic material:

- Chromosomal DNA
- Plasmids


Important concepts:

- Mutation
- Gene transfer
- Genetic adaptation


---

# 9. Horizontal Gene Transfer (HGT)

Horizontal gene transfer is the movement of genetic material between organisms without reproduction.


Major mechanisms:


## Transformation

Uptake of free DNA from the environment.


## Transduction

DNA transfer mediated by bacteriophages.


## Conjugation

Direct DNA transfer between bacterial cells through cell contact.


Importance:

- Evolution of bacteria
- Antibiotic resistance spread


---

# 10. Microbial Mutation and Variation

Mutations create genetic diversity in microorganisms.

Types:

- Point mutation
- Insertion
- Deletion


Effects:

- Altered protein function
- Antibiotic resistance
- Adaptation


---

# 11. Virology

**Virology** is the study of viruses.

Viruses are acellular infectious agents that require host cells for replication.


Basic virus components:

- Genetic material (DNA or RNA)
- Capsid protein
- Envelope (some viruses)


---

Viruses can contain:

| Genome Type | Example |
|---|---|

| ssDNA | Parvovirus |
| dsRNA | Rotavirus |
| ssRNA | SARS-CoV-2 |

# 14. SARS-CoV-2 Biology

SARS-CoV-2 is an enveloped positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus.

Important features:

- Genome type: +ssRNA
- Family: Coronaviridae
- Host receptor: ACE2


Viral components:

- Spike protein (S)
- Membrane protein (M)
- Envelope protein (E)
- Nucleocapsid protein (N)


Importance in bioinformatics:

- Viral genome analysis
- Mutation tracking
- Host response analysis
- Transcriptomics studies


---

# 15. Host-Microbe Interaction

Microorganisms interact with hosts through:

- Colonization
- Infection
- Immune response


Important concepts:

## Pathogen

A microorganism capable of causing disease.


## Virulence

The ability of a microorganism to cause disease.


## Virulence Factors

Microbial features that promote infection.

Examples:

- Toxins
- Adhesion molecules
- Immune evasion mechanisms


---

# 16. Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)

Antimicrobial resistance occurs when microorganisms survive exposure to antimicrobial drugs.


Mechanisms:

## Enzyme Production

Example:

- β-lactamase production


## Target Modification

Changes drug target molecules.


## Efflux Pumps

Remove antibiotics from cells.


## Reduced Permeability

Limits drug entry.


---

# 17. Antibiotic Resistance Genes

Resistance genes can spread through:

- Plasmids
- Transposons
- Horizontal gene transfer


Bioinformatics applications:

- AMR gene identification
- Resistance prediction
- Microbial genome analysis

# 19. Metagenomics

Metagenomics studies genetic material directly from environmental or clinical samples without culturing individual organisms.


Applications:

- Microbiome analysis
- Environmental studies
- Pathogen detection


Types:

## Amplicon Sequencing

Example:

- 16S rRNA sequencing


## Shotgun Metagenomics

Sequences all DNA present in a sample.
