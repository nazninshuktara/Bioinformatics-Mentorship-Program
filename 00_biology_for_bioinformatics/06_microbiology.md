# Microbiology

## 1. Overview & Scope

**Microbiology** is the branch of biology that studies microorganisms, including bacteria, viruses, fungi, and other microscopic organisms.

Microorganisms play important roles in:

- Human health and disease
- Environment
- Biotechnology
- Agriculture
- Evolution
- Industrial processes


**Scope:**

- Microbial structure and classification
- Microbial growth and metabolism
- Microbial genetics
- Host-microbe interactions
- Pathogenesis
- Antimicrobial resistance
- Microbial genomics
- Metagenomics


**Applications:**

- Infectious disease research
- Vaccine development
- Antibiotic discovery
- Pathogen identification
- Microbiome research
- Bioinformatics analysis

# 3. Bacteria

Bacteria are single-celled prokaryotic organisms without a membrane-bound nucleus.

General features:

- Circular chromosome
- 70S ribosome
- Cell wall containing peptidoglycan
- Reproduce mainly by binary fission


Basic bacterial structure:
```
Capsule
   ↓
Cell Wall
   ↓
Cell Membrane
   ↓
Cytoplasm
   ↓
Nucleoid DNA
```


---

# 4. Bacterial Cell Structure

## Cell Wall

Functions:

- Maintains cell shape
- Provides protection
- Prevents osmotic damage


## Cell Membrane

Functions:

- Controls transport
- Energy production
- Signal exchange


## Capsule

A protective outer layer made of polysaccharides.

Functions:

- Helps bacterial survival
- Protects against immune responses


## Flagella

Structures responsible for bacterial movement.


## Pili / Fimbriae

Functions:

- Attachment to host cells
- DNA transfer during conjugation


## Plasmids

Small circular DNA molecules separate from bacterial chromosome.

Functions:

- Carry antibiotic resistance genes
- Transfer genetic information


---

# 5. Bacterial Classification

Bacteria are classified based on:

- Cell morphology
- Staining characteristics
- Genetic relationship


## Based on Shape

| Shape | Example |
|---|---|
| Cocci | *Staphylococcus* |
| Bacilli | *E. coli* |
| Spiral | *Treponema* |


## Gram Staining

Gram staining differentiates bacteria based on cell wall structure.


### Gram-positive Bacteria

Characteristics:

- Thick peptidoglycan layer
- Retain crystal violet stain


### Gram-negative Bacteria

Characteristics:

- Thin peptidoglycan layer
- Outer membrane present
- Contains lipopolysaccharide (LPS)

- Chromosomal DNA

- Mutation

---

# 9. Horizontal Gene Transfer (HGT)

Horizontal gene transfer is the movement of genetic material between organisms without reproduction.


Major mechanisms:


## Transformation

Uptake of free DNA from the environment.


## Transduction

DNA transfer mediated by bacteriophages.


## Conjugation

Direct DNA transfer between bacterial cells through cell contact.


Importance:

- Evolution of bacteria
- Antibiotic resistance spread


---

# 10. Microbial Mutation and Variation

Mutations create genetic diversity in microorganisms.

Types:

- Point mutation
- Insertion
- Deletion


Effects:

- Altered protein function
- Antibiotic resistance
- Adaptation

# 11. Virology

**Virology** is the study of viruses.

Viruses are acellular infectious agents that require host cells for replication.


Basic virus components:

- Genetic material (DNA or RNA)
- Capsid protein
- Envelope (some viruses)

# 16. Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)

Antimicrobial resistance occurs when microorganisms survive exposure to antimicrobial drugs.


Mechanisms:

## Enzyme Production

Example:

- β-lactamase production


## Target Modification

Changes drug target molecules.


## Efflux Pumps

Remove antibiotics from cells.


## Reduced Permeability

Limits drug entry.

## Practical 4 — Gram Staining and Microscopic Examination

### Aim

To differentiate bacteria into Gram-positive and Gram-negative groups.

### Principle

Gram staining differentiates bacteria based on cell-wall structure.

- Gram-positive bacteria have a thick peptidoglycan layer.
- Gram-negative bacteria have a thin peptidoglycan layer and an outer membrane.

### Main Staining Reagents

| Reagent | Role |
|---|---|
| Crystal violet | Primary stain |
| Iodine | Mordant |
| Decolorizer | Removes stain from Gram-negative cells |
| Safranin | Counterstain |

### Interpretation

| Result | Appearance | General interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Gram-positive | Purple | Retains crystal violet |
| Gram-negative | Pink or red | Takes up counterstain |

### Other Observations

- Cocci
- Bacilli
- Spiral-shaped bacteria
- Cell arrangement
- Presence of contamination

## Practical 6 — Biochemical Identification of Bacteria

### Aim

To identify bacteria using biochemical characteristics.

### Principle

Different bacterial species have different metabolic enzymes and biochemical activities.

### Common Tests

| Test | What it indicates |
|---|---|
| Catalase test | Ability to break down hydrogen peroxide |
| Oxidase test | Presence of cytochrome oxidase |
| Indole test | Tryptophan metabolism |
| Citrate test | Ability to use citrate as a carbon source |
| Urease test | Ability to break down urea |
| Carbohydrate fermentation test | Ability to ferment specific sugars |

### General Workflow

```text
Pure bacterial culture
 ↓
Biochemical tests
 ↓
Observation of reactions
 ↓
Comparison with identification profile
 ↓
Probable bacterial identification
```

---

## Practical 7 — Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing

### Aim

To assess whether a bacterial isolate is susceptible, intermediate, or resistant to selected antimicrobial agents.

### Principle

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing measures bacterial growth response in the presence of antimicrobial agents.

### Key Topics

- Disk-diffusion method concept
- Zone of inhibition
- Susceptible, intermediate, and resistant categories
- Quality control
- Importance of standardized interpretation

### Interpretation

| Observation | General meaning |
|---|---|
| Large zone of inhibition | Greater susceptibility under standardized conditions |
| Small or absent zone | Reduced susceptibility or resistance may be present |

> Results must be interpreted according to approved laboratory standards and quality-control procedures.

### Aim

To identify bacteria using DNA-based methods.

### Principle

Bacterial DNA is extracted and a conserved genetic marker, commonly the 16S rRNA gene, can be amplified and sequenced for identification.

```text
 ↓
PCR product purification
 ↓
Sanger sequencing
 ↓
Sequence comparison
 ↓
Probable bacterial identification
```

### Important Concepts
- Bacterial genomic DNA extraction
- PCR

- 16S rRNA gene
- DNA sequencing
