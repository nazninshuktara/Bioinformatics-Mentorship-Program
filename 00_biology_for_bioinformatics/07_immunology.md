# Immunology

## 1. Overview & Scope

**Immunology** is the branch of biology that studies the immune system and its role in protecting the body from pathogens, abnormal cells, and other foreign substances.

The immune system recognizes and responds to:

- Bacteria
- Viruses
- Fungi
- Parasites
- Abnormal or damaged cells

**Scope:**

- Immune system organization
- Innate immunity
- Adaptive immunity
- Immune cells
- Antibodies
- Cytokines
- Antigen recognition
- Inflammation
- Immune memory
- Host-pathogen interaction

**Applications:**

- Infectious disease research
- Vaccine development
- Cancer immunology
- Autoimmune disease research
- Immunotherapy
- Biomarker discovery
- Transcriptomics and bioinformatics


---

# 2. Immune System

The **immune system** is a network of cells, tissues, organs, and molecules that protects the body from harmful agents.

Major components:

- Immune cells
- Lymphoid organs
- Cytokines
- Antibodies
- Complement proteins


## Primary Lymphoid Organs

Sites where immune cells develop and mature.

- Bone marrow
- Thymus


## Secondary Lymphoid Organs

Sites where immune responses are initiated.

- Lymph nodes
- Spleen
- Mucosal lymphoid tissues


---

# 3. Innate and Adaptive Immunity

The immune system is broadly divided into:

```text
Immune System
      |
      ├── Innate Immunity
      |
      └── Adaptive Immunity
``` id="4y7k1q"


| Feature | Innate Immunity | Adaptive Immunity |
|---|---|---|
| Response | Rapid | Slower initially |
| Specificity | Broad | Highly specific |
| Memory | Limited | Strong immune memory |
| Major cells | Neutrophils, macrophages, NK cells | B cells, T cells |


---

# 4. Innate Immunity

Innate immunity provides the first line of defense against pathogens.

Major components:

- Physical barriers
- Chemical barriers
- Phagocytic cells
- Natural killer cells
- Complement system
- Inflammatory response


## Physical Barriers

Examples:

- Skin
- Mucus
- Respiratory epithelium


## Chemical Barriers

Examples:

- Lysozyme
- Acidic environments
- Antimicrobial peptides


---

# 5. Innate Immune Cells

## Neutrophils

Functions:

- Rapid response to infection
- Phagocytosis
- Microbial killing


## Macrophages

Functions:

- Phagocytosis
- Antigen presentation
- Cytokine production


## Dendritic Cells

Functions:

- Capture antigens
- Antigen presentation
- Activate T cells


## Natural Killer (NK) Cells

Functions:

- Kill infected cells
- Kill abnormal cells
- Produce immune-regulatory cytokines


## Monocytes

Circulating immune cells that can differentiate into macrophages and other cell types in tissues.


---

# 6. Adaptive Immunity

Adaptive immunity provides antigen-specific immune responses.

Major components:

- B lymphocytes
- T lymphocytes
- Antibodies


Characteristics:

- Antigen specificity
- Clonal expansion
- Immune memory


---

# 7. B Cells

B cells are lymphocytes responsible for humoral immunity.

Major functions:

- Antibody production
- Antigen presentation
- Immune memory


B-cell differentiation:

```text
B Cell
   ↓
Plasma Cell
   ↓
Antibody Production
``` id="zq0jzq"


## Memory B Cells

Remain after an immune response and contribute to faster responses upon re-exposure to the same antigen.


---

# 8. T Cells

T cells are lymphocytes involved in cell-mediated immunity.

Major types:


## Helper T Cells (CD4+)

Functions:

- Coordinate immune responses
- Activate B cells
- Activate macrophages
- Produce cytokines


## Cytotoxic T Cells (CD8+)

Functions:

- Recognize infected or abnormal cells
- Kill target cells


## Regulatory T Cells (Treg)

Functions:

- Suppress excessive immune responses
- Maintain immune tolerance


---

# 9. Antigens and Antibodies

## Antigen

A molecule that can be specifically recognized by components of the adaptive immune system.


## Antibody

An immunoglobulin protein produced by B-cell-derived plasma cells that specifically binds an antigen.


Major antibody classes:

| Antibody | Major Role |
|---|---|
| IgG | Major antibody in blood and tissues |
| IgM | Early antibody response |
| IgA | Mucosal immunity |
| IgE | Allergy and parasite responses |
| IgD | B-cell-associated functions |


---

# 10. Antigen Presentation

Antigen presentation allows immune cells to recognize peptides derived from pathogens or abnormal cells.


Major molecules:

- MHC class I
- MHC class II


## MHC Class I

Presents intracellularly derived peptides to:

- CD8+ T cells


## MHC Class II

Presents extracellularly derived peptides to:

- CD4+ T cells


General concept:

```text
Antigen
   ↓
Processing
   ↓
MHC Presentation
   ↓
T Cell Recognition
``` id="3r9x5k"


---

# 11. T Cell Receptor (TCR)

The **T-cell receptor (TCR)** recognizes antigen-derived peptides presented by MHC molecules.

Recognition requires:

```text
Peptide + MHC
       ↓
      TCR
       ↓
T Cell Activation
``` id="t0b8r8"


TCR specificity is important for adaptive immune responses.


---

# 12. Cytokines

**Cytokines** are signaling molecules that regulate immune and inflammatory responses.

Important cytokine groups:

- Interleukins (IL)
- Interferons (IFN)
- Tumor necrosis factors (TNF)
- Chemokines


Examples:

| Cytokine | Major Role |
|---|---|
| IL-1 | Inflammation |
| IL-6 | Inflammation and acute-phase response |
| TNF | Inflammation |
| IFN-α/β | Antiviral response |
| IFN-γ | Immune activation |
| IL-10 | Immune regulation |


---

# 13. Chemokines

Chemokines are signaling proteins that guide immune cell movement.

They regulate:

- Immune cell recruitment
- Migration
- Tissue localization


Example:

```text
Infection
   ↓
Chemokine production
   ↓
Immune cell recruitment
   ↓
Site of infection
``` id="91h7sq"


---

# 14. Inflammation

**Inflammation** is a protective immune response to infection, tissue damage, or other harmful stimuli.

General process:

```text
Damage / Infection
        ↓
Immune recognition
        ↓
Cytokine & chemokine release
        ↓
Immune cell recruitment
        ↓
Inflammatory response
        ↓
Resolution / Repair
``` id="9e5kcb"


Important inflammatory mediators:

- IL-1
- IL-6
- TNF
- Chemokines


---

# 15. Complement System

The complement system is a group of proteins that supports immune defense.

Major functions:

- Pathogen recognition
- Opsonization
- Inflammation
- Membrane attack complex formation


Three major activation pathways:

- Classical pathway
- Lectin pathway
- Alternative pathway


---

# 16. Pattern Recognition Receptors (PRRs)

PRRs recognize conserved molecular patterns associated with pathogens or cellular damage.


Examples:

- Toll-like receptors (TLRs)
- RIG-I-like receptors (RLRs)
- NOD-like receptors (NLRs)


Recognized molecules include:

- Viral RNA
- Bacterial components
- Damaged-cell signals


General concept:

```text
Pathogen-associated signal
          ↓
        PRR
          ↓
Cell signaling
          ↓
Cytokine / Interferon response
``` id="p4x8kf"


---

# 17. Antiviral Immunity

The immune system uses several mechanisms to control viral infections.

Major components:

- Type I interferons
- NK cells
- CD8+ T cells
- Antibodies
- Dendritic cells


General response:

```text
Viral Infection
      ↓
Viral Recognition
      ↓
Interferon Response
      ↓
Innate Immune Activation
      ↓
Adaptive Immune Response
      ↓
Viral Control / Clearance
``` id="gqf8py"


---

# 18. Immune Memory

After an adaptive immune response, some B and T cells remain as memory cells.

Upon re-exposure:

```text
Previous Exposure
      ↓
Memory B / T Cells
      ↓
Faster Response
      ↓
Enhanced Protection
``` id="4h9j4x"


Immune memory is the basis of long-term protection provided by many vaccines.


---

# 19. Vaccination

Vaccination stimulates an immune response without causing the disease in the same way as natural infection.

General concept:

```text
Vaccine Antigen
      ↓
Immune Activation
      ↓
B / T Cell Response
      ↓
Memory Cells
      ↓
Faster Future Response
``` id="y3q2bs"


Types include:

- Live attenuated vaccines
- Inactivated vaccines
- Protein/subunit vaccines
- Viral vector vaccines
- mRNA vaccines


---

# 20. Immune Dysregulation

Abnormal immune responses can contribute to disease.

Examples:

## Autoimmunity

The immune system reacts against the body's own components.


## Allergy

Excessive immune response to otherwise harmless substances.


## Immunodeficiency

Reduced or impaired immune function.


## Excessive Inflammation

Uncontrolled inflammatory signaling can contribute to tissue damage.

# Required Practical - Immunology

## Practical 1 — PBMC Isolation, Cell Counting, and Viability Assessment

### Aim

To isolate peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), determine cell concentration, and assess cell viability.

### Principle

PBMCs include:

- Lymphocytes
- Monocytes

After isolation, cells are counted and viability is assessed before downstream immunology experiments.

### Key Topics

- PBMC concept
- Sterile sample handling
- Cell counting
- Hemocytometer or automated cell counter
- Trypan blue exclusion
- Cell viability calculation
- Cell storage and labelling

### Cell Viability Formula

```text
Cell viability (%) = Viable cells / Total cells × 100
```
---
## Practical 2 — Antigen-Antibody Reaction and ELISA

### Aim
To detect or quantify an antigen or antibody using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay.

### Principle

ELISA is based on specific binding between an antigen and an antibody.

```
Antigen or antibody
 ↓
Specific antibody-antigen binding
 ↓
Enzyme-linked detection
 ↓
Colour development
 ↓
Absorbance measurement
```

### Main ELISA Types

| ELISA Type | Main Use |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Direct ELISA** | Detects antigen using labelled primary antibody |
| **Indirect ELISA** | Detects antibody using labelled secondary antibody |
| **Sandwich ELISA** | Detects antigen between capture and detection antibodies |
| **Competitive ELISA** | Measures competition between sample and labelled antigen or antibody |

### Applications
* Antibody detection
* Antigen detection
* Cytokine measurement
* Vaccine-response studies
* Biomarker analysis

---

## Practical 3 — Flow Cytometry and Immune-Cell Immunophenotyping

### Aim
To identify and quantify immune-cell populations using fluorescently labelled antibodies.

### Principle
* Flow cytometry analyzes individual cells as they pass through a laser beam.
* Fluorescent antibodies bind to specific cell-surface or intracellular markers.

### Important Immune Markers

| Marker | Common Cell Population |
| :--- | :--- |
| **CD3** | T cells |
| **CD4** | Helper T cells |
| **CD8** | Cytotoxic T cells |
| **CD19 or CD20** | B cells |
| **CD14** | Monocytes |
| **CD56** | Natural killer cells |

### General Workflow
```
Cell suspension
 ↓
Antibody staining
 ↓
Flow cytometer acquisition
 ↓
Gating strategy
 ↓
Immune-cell population analysis
```

### Key Outputs
* Percentage of each immune-cell population
* Cell-marker expression
* Cell viability
* Cell activation-marker expression

| Method | Use |

## Practical 7 — Immune Gene Expression Analysis by RT-qPCR

### Aim
To measure expression of immune-related genes using RT-qPCR.

### Principle
RNA is extracted from immune cells or tissues and converted to cDNA. RT-qPCR measures expression of selected immune genes.

```
Immune cells or tissue
 ↓
RNA extraction
 ↓
cDNA synthesis
 ↓
RT-qPCR
 ↓
Ct or Cq analysis
 ↓
Relative gene expression
```

### Example Immune Genes

| Gene Group | Examples |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Interferon response** | IFNA, IFNB, ISG15, MX1 |
| **Inflammatory cytokines** | IL1B, IL6, TNF |
| **Chemokines** | CXCL10, CCL2 |
| **T-cell markers** | CD3D, CD4, CD8A |
| **Antiviral response** | OAS1, IFIT1, IFITM3 |

### Data Interpretation
* $\Delta Ct = Ct(\text{target}) - Ct(\text{reference})$
* $\Delta\Delta Ct = \Delta Ct(\text{sample}) - \Delta Ct(\text{control})$
* $\text{Relative expression} = 2^{-\Delta\Delta Ct}$

### Applications
* Viral infection studies
* Inflammation studies
* Immune-response profiling
* Vaccine-response analysis
* Transcriptomics result validation
